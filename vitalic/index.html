I · THE EQUATION

The original framework proposes that civilizational complexity (C) grows according to:

dC/dt = α(S × I) + β(C × D) − γC²

Where S = Selection Pressure (competition, merit, contestability), I = Integration Capacity (trust, synthesis, mobility, tolerance), and γC² = entropic drag that grows quadratically with accumulated complexity.

The data performed surgery on this. Selection pressure alone has a negative coefficient with complexity change. The revised equation elevates Integration to primary driver:

dC/dt = α·I·(1 + β·Sc) + μ(I)·C − γC² − δ·Sd + ρ·Cmem

Sc = constructive selection (merit, exams, competition). Sd = destructive selection (war, purges). μ(I)·C = maintenance capacity. ρ·Cmem = civilizational memory.

III · ENTROPY IS UNIVERSAL

Two empires separated by 1,700 years. Same entropic signature. The denarius lost 94% of its silver content over 500 years. Sterling lost 97% of its purchasing power over 300 years. The physics is the same: when maintenance cost exceeds creative output, the state eats its own monetary infrastructure.

Denarius Quadratic Fit
R² = 0.943
Roman denarius silver content from 97% (211 BCE) to 3% (275 CE). The quadratic acceleration confirms the γC² drag term.

Entropy Channel Decomposition

Rome's decline operated through four channels simultaneously: material (debasement), institutional (inequality ratchet), reproductive (population collapse from 61M to 25M), and spiritual (literary output collapse).

Honest negative: The entropy ratchet hypothesis (entropy can only decrease via regime change) is consistent with the data but has not been formally tested. The quadratic vs linear vs exponential fits for debasement are nearly indistinguishable (R² = 0.941, 0.943, 0.942). The quadratic acceleration is real but marginal.

IV · INTEGRATION DOES THE HEAVY LIFTING

The original framework gives S and I equal weight. The data says Integration does ~65% of the work. European homicide rates declined from 14.7 per 100,000 in 1250 to 0.8 by 1912, a 94% drop that tracks complexity growth almost perfectly. Lower violence = higher trust = higher integration = higher complexity.

Dutch Republic — Strongest Single-Civ Result
r = 0.780, p = 0.003
Bond yields fell from 8% to 2.5% as complexity rose. Yields are a trust proxy. Tolerance literally = wealth.
England — Monotonic Integration Rise
r = 0.867, p < 0.001
Homicide decline + market integration + literacy rise track complexity almost perfectly over 600 years.

Pooled Regression

Critical finding: In the pooled regression, S alone has a negative coefficient (−0.52, p=0.027). High selection pressure more often destroys complexity than builds it. The framework needs to distinguish constructive selection (merit, competition, exams) from destructive selection (war, purges, plague). Only Sc paired with I builds. Sd destroys regardless.

Peak civilizations cluster in the upper-right of S-I space (high integration, moderate-to-high constructive selection). Crisis points cluster at bottom-right (high destructive S, collapsed I). Tokugawa sits alone at left (low S, moderate I = maintenance without growth).

VI · THEORY REVISIONS

Seven proposed revisions were formally tested against data. Four strongly confirmed.

S_c/S_d Decomposition — THE KEY FINDING
Sd = −0.15, p = 0.002
The negative S coefficient was driven entirely by destructive pressure (war, purges). Constructive S (merit, competition) is near-zero. R² improves 0.34→0.44 with decomposition.
min(S,I) > S×I — Liebig's Law
AIC 435 vs 441
Complexity growth is limited by the weakest link, not the product. A civilization with high S but low I is constrained by the bottleneck.
