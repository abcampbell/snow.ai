Thesis
Affirm is a subprime lender disguised as fintech, generating 50% of revenue from 20-36% APR loans to fragile borrowers. Trump's proposed 10% credit card rate cap threatens its model. Rising unemployment, thin 2% reserves, 6x+ leverage, and normalized 6-7% losses will collapse earnings and drive stock to book value of $9.

Catalysts

  • Trump administration 10% APR cap on credit cards extending to BNPL
  • Rising unemployment from 4.0% to 4.6%+ hitting subprime borrowers first
  • Student loan payments resuming September 2025 after SAVE forbearance expires
  • Credit normalization driving losses from 3% to 6-7% of retained risk
  • Securitization market tightening requiring higher first-loss retention
  • Merchant discount rate compression from intensifying competition

Red Flags Alleged

  • Regulatory arbitrage via Utah rent-a-bank structure to charge 36% APRs bypassing state usury caps
  • Losses on loan purchase commitments not labeled as credit losses but economically equivalent
  • Fragmented securitization reporting that stops when loans are most vulnerable to default
  • Hidden leverage above 6x when off-balance-sheet retained risk is included
  • Credit reserves of only 2% of retained risk versus 6-7% normalized losses
  • 96% of transactions from repeat users indicating borrower saturation not expansion
  • Loan stacking across BNPL providers invisible due to limited credit bureau reporting
  • Financing shift from discretionary items to necessities like groceries indicating customer stress
