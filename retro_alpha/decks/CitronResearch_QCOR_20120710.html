Thesis
Questcor is a scheme, not a business, marketing a 60-year-old drug (Acthar) at inflated prices. Single drug entirely vulnerable to generic/synthetic competition, insurance pushback, and no IP protection. Insiders selling heavily while buying back shares. Stock headed to single digits within 18 months.

Catalysts

  • Generic ACTH competitor entry (market threshold already exceeded at $50-100M annual revenues vs current $400M+)
  • Novartis Synacthen Depot (synthetic ACTH) US market entry - selling at 1/30th Acthar price in Europe
  • Insurance company reimbursement restrictions (Blue Cross, UnitedHealthcare policies already tightening)
  • New MS drug candidates approaching FDA approval in Q4 2012
  • DOJ or FDA regulatory action against off-label marketing abuses

Red Flags Alleged

  • No IP protection: 'The patent for Acthar has expired and we have no intellectual property-based market exclusivity'
  • Orphan drug abuse: Infantile Spasms (orphan designation) now only 6-10% of revenue; marketing heavily for other indications at orphan-justified pricing
  • Fraudulent barrier-to-entry claims: CEO claims 'undisclosed composition' and 'secret sauce' for 60-year-old drug extracted from pig pituitaries using standard purification
  • Rheumatology expansion based on unblinded 5-patient retrospective study with no control arm
  • Terminated MS efficacy trial (NCT00947895) after poor enrollment, continues marketing anyway
  • Massive insider selling: $120M sold in 12 months vs only $1,500 purchased; 2.2M shares sold
  • Share buybacks disguise option dilution: 87% of operating cash flow spent on buybacks that merely offset 2.5M annual option grants to insiders
  • Marketing expense abuse: $13.8M quarterly in unexplained non-salary marketing ($3,357-3,883 per vial) suggests speaker fees and incentives to physicians
  • Minimal R&D: $16.8M in 2011 vs $120M insider selling; no pipeline, no new drug candidates
