Thesis
Canadian housing bubble has burst; MIC faces balance sheet wipeout in crash scenario or 66% earnings decline in soft landing due to aggressive revenue recognition, risky underwriting, and exposure to overleveraged borrowers with high actual LTVs.

Catalysts

  • 50% of Canadian mortgages up for renewal in 2018
  • B-20 regulation reducing affordability by 20%
  • Rising interest rates (Bank of Canada projected to raise rates twice in 2018)
  • New rent control and Airbnb restrictions in major cities
  • Prices down 10% nationally and 14% in Toronto from peak

Red Flags Alleged

  • Over 80% of premiums recorded as revenue in first 5 years despite long-tail risk
  • Only $119mil loss reserves against $218bil outstanding mortgages insured
  • $97bil insured mortgages with 'actual LTV' greater than 90%
  • 85% of applications approved, most underwriting decisions in 4 hours
  • No loss reserves until borrower 90 days delinquent
  • Premiums based only on LTV, no individualized pricing for credit score, location, dwelling type, etc.
  • Banks have no skin-in-the-game creating perverse incentives and adverse selection
  • Only 271 employees for entire underwriting operation
  • Homeownership Assistance Program interventions (56% of delinquencies) not reported as delinquencies, hiding true default rates

Key Risks Acknowledged

  • Government intervention could roll back regulatory changes or BOC could cut rates
  • Negative carry: dividend yield of 4.6%, cost to borrow of 2.5%
  • Borrow rate cost could spike dramatically (to 100%) as happened with HCG and EQB
  • Bull case: company insures first time homebuyers at price points well below average, cannot insure properties >$1mil avoiding some excesses
