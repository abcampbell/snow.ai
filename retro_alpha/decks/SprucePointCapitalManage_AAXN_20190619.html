Thesis
Axon, the Taser stun gun maker repositioning as SaaS provider, will disappoint with significant earnings misses and increasing cash burn. Aggressive revenue recognition has pulled forward sales, undisclosed China tariff exposure will compress margins, and the company will fail to scale beyond niche businesses. Material weaknesses, SEC issues, and flawed acquisitions support expectations of 40-60% downside.

Catalysts

  • Earnings disappointment from gross margin pressure due to China tariffs
  • Q1 2019 operating cash burn worst in public history signals ongoing financial challenges
  • Failure to monetize Records and AI products in H2 2019
  • Inability to penetrate CAD/RMS markets against entrenched competitors
  • Rising video storage costs pressuring Software and Sensors margins
  • VIEVU acquisition failure becoming more apparent

Red Flags Alleged

  • Aggressive revenue recognition: booking hardware revenue upfront when devices have no standalone value without software, inflating revenues by 6-9%
  • Material weaknesses in revenue recognition and cost of goods sold controls identified in 2016
  • Total receivables grew 456% while revenues grew only 99% from 2014-2018, suggesting premature revenue recognition
  • Undisclosed compensation accounting errors dating back to 2014 in 401k filings
  • Concealing China import dependence: removed word 'China' from 2018 10-K despite 56% of 2019 shipments coming from China
  • Senseless $234m equity raise in June 2018 with no defined purpose while holding $96m cash and no debt
  • VIEVU acquisition appears failed: 70% inventory reserve on acquired inventory, contingent consideration liability written to zero by year-end 2018
  • Poor capex planning: missed 2018 guidance by 20%, quietly increased new HQ cost by 30%
  • Stopped disclosing work-in-progress inventory while increasing reserves every year
  • Quarterly cloud revenue disclosures don't reconcile to annual totals
  • Allegations of bribing police chiefs to win contracts
  • Audit Committee Chairman was Arthur Andersen partner during Enron era
  • EVP Worldwide Products left after less than 2 years; Technology Committee Chair resigned June 2019
  • CFO transition, 10-K delay, multiple unanswered SEC comment letters
