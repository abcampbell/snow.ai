Thesis
Cambrex is a smaller API contract manufacturer in a commodity business, with >35% revenue and likely >50% operating earnings from Gilead's declining HCV franchise. Loss of this high-margin business will cause sales declines, operating deleverage, margin compression, and multiple compression.

Catalysts

  • Firm commitment for significantly reduced Gilead volumes in 2018
  • 5-year supply agreement ending December 2020 with defined minimum volume for 2019 representing further significant decline
  • No minimum volume stipulated for 2020
  • First take-or-pay payment ($6.2 million) booked in Q4 2017 since 2003

Red Flags Alleged

  • Management selling stock: COO sold $4.5 million since 2016, former CFO sold $4.4 million since 2016
  • CEO sold $11.98 million in stock since 2016 while current holdings only $4.57 million
  • 82 insider open market sells versus only 2 buys since 2012
  • First take-or-pay payment since 2003 suggests customer (likely Gilead) not taking contracted volumes
  • Sell-side expectations wildly inflated: to hit guidance midpoint requires 10.8% ex-Gilead sales growth, 330 bps higher than 2014-2017 CAGR

Key Risks Acknowledged

  • Cambrex replaces Gilead HCV franchise with a similarly remarkable product (though likelihood is low given Gilead's HCV is most successful drug launch ever)
  • Gilead's decline allows Cambrex to focus on other opportunities (replacements will take time and likely have lower margins)
  • Cambrex is acquired for a hefty premium (doesn't make sense given trading at market multiple on peak margins)
