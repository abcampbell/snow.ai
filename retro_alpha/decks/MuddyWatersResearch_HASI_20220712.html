Thesis
HASI's accounting is so complex and misleading that financial statements are effectively meaningless. Most income is non-cash and unrealizable through three mechanisms: 1) booking non-cash unrealizable income from third parties' tax credits that will reverse; 2) inflating gains on securitizations via implausibly low discount rates; 3) booking non-cash PIK interest from stressed borrowers.

Red Flags Alleged

  • Books non-cash unrealizable income from third parties' tax credits via HLBV accounting loophole that will reverse over 20+ years
  • Manipulates discount rates on securitization residuals to implausibly low levels (4.3% in 2021 vs 8-10% in 2013) despite shift to riskier commercial receivables
  • Records non-cash PIK interest income from stressed borrowers as revenue
  • $502 million in loans to 49%-owned EMI SunStrong not disclosed as related party transactions
  • SunStrong has cumulative losses since 2018 yet HASI records significant income through non-cash HLBV accounting
  • Refinances SunStrong loans with nearly identical terms to create appearance of loan repayment and inflate investing cash flows
  • Convoluted HA Helix conveyance to SunStrong appears designed to inappropriately record operating cash flow
  • Distributable Earnings metric includes non-cash income components, misleading investors about cash generation
  • 91% of dividends financed through capital raises rather than internally generated cash flows since IPO
  • Uses non-cash HLBV income to inflate operating cash flow by counting capital distributions as OCF
  • CEO liquidated or gifted $17.1 million in stock over two years; executives disposed of $22.9 million total
  • EMIs reported approximately $449 million in cumulative losses 2019-2021 while HASI booked $238.6 million in profit from same EMIs
  • Tangible Common Equity to Tangible Managed Assets ratio adjusted from 36.7% to 29.4%, approaching sub-investment grade threshold
