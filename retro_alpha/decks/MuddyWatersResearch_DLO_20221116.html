Red Flags Alleged

  • Conflicting TPV disclosures: 2019 cohort TPV changed from $471m to $56m, 2020 cohort from $343m to $260m without explanation
  • Conflicting receivable disclosures: Foreign currency receivables differed by 10% in same F-1 document
  • Subsidiary account conflicts: UK LLP showed $15.7m payable to Malta Operating vs Malta Operating showing only $12.4m receivable from all related parties
  • Fraudulent accounting to disguise option exercise timing and funding: Changed accounts to claim November 2020 loan was never funded and options exercised in March/April 2021 instead of December 2020
  • Cash flow reconciliation deficit of $3.3m at consolidated level and $4.1m at Malta Operating level, suggesting possible use of client funds
  • Take Rate grew 17.5% in 2020 despite conditions implying it should have decreased
  • FX gains appear roughly double to triple expected levels: ~3% vs expected 1.1-1.5% of cross-border TPV
  • Highly manual backend processes relying on spreadsheets
  • Malta subsidiary breached Tier 1 capital requirements in both 2020 and 2021
  • Appears to be dodging FCA regulation through lack of physical presence exemption despite material UK operations
  • Questionable auditor choices: PwC Argentina for consolidated, MHA MacIntyre Hudson for UK entities which also audits AstroPay entities
  • Ongoing entanglements with former parent AstroPay which processes payments for high-risk merchants in potentially illegal activities
  • Related party collection agents handled 5% of 2020 TPV and 3% of 2021 TPV
  • Insiders sold ~$1 billion in shares within first five months of IPO
  • Spate of recent senior executive departures including COO, CTO, Malta MD, VP Finance, and others
