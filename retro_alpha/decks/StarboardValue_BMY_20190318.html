Thesis
Bristol-Myers' $91B Celgene acquisition is ill-advised and value-destructive. Management ascribes ~$30B to Celgene's pipeline (not ~$15B as implied), requiring unprecedented success—10 blockbusters in 8 years versus Celgene's historical 3 in 15 years. Even achieving base case generates only 3% IRR above WACC. Standalone Bristol-Myers with operational improvements offers superior risk-adjusted returns.

Catalysts

  • Shareholders vote down Celgene acquisition (only $40 million reimbursement fee)
  • Continue String of Pearls strategy with ~$37 billion cumulative unlevered FCF over 5 years for partnerships, licenses, small acquisitions
  • Operational improvements targeting ~900 bps margin expansion
  • OPDIVO franchise remains stable and growing with secular tailwinds in immuno-oncology

Red Flags Alleged

  • Bristol-Myers completed only 2 weeks of full due diligence on ~25 Celgene pipeline compounds for a $91 billion transaction
  • Deal process appears rushed with arbitrary deadline despite Celgene's willingness to allow more time
  • Celgene has terminated or de-prioritized ~30% of products and >$5.5 billion peak sales highlighted only 2 years ago
  • Three of five highlighted near-term Celgene products have been delayed in just the last 2 years
  • Management misleadingly implies ~$15 billion pipeline value when actual cost is ~$30 billion after proper synergy allocation
  • Deal may have been done for defensive purposes to protect Bristol-Myers from becoming an acquisition target
  • REVLIMID represents 63% of Celgene 2018 revenue and faces 2026 patent cliff—among largest in pharma history
  • Management assumes cost synergies create impossible scenario where EBITDA exceeds revenue for marketed products post-2024
  • Base case requires Celgene to produce 10 blockbusters in 8 years versus historical 3 blockbusters in 15 years
