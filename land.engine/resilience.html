Best land to own

The same test as “The World’s Best Land to Own” (Mediterranean regions, resilience against what a hectare costs), run on every US county from public data. Resilience averages five scores, each a percentile among US counties: heat and water stress in 2050, wildfire, floods, storms and quakes, and how well the community recovers. Price is USDA farmland. Dashed lines are the US medians, so each quadrant is relative to the rest of the country.

How the score is built

Five parts, each ranked against every other county (100 = the most resilient county in the country, 0 = the least), then averaged. Toggle parts above to rescore.

  • Heat 2050: days a year above 95°F by mid-century (RCP4.5, LOCA-downscaled climate models), fewer is better.
  • Water 2050: water stress in 2050 under business as usual (WRI Aqueduct 4.0, 0–5), area-weighted from watersheds to the county. Arid, low-use basins count as extremely stressed, as Aqueduct scores them.
  • Wildfire: FEMA National Risk Index wildfire loss rate (expected annual loss over what's exposed), as a national percentile.
  • Floods, storms, quakes: FEMA loss rates on buildings from inland and coastal flooding, hurricanes, earthquakes and tornadoes, summed.
  • Community: FEMA's community resilience score (University of South Carolina BRIC): the social, economic and institutional capacity to recover.

What's different from the original

The original's five are climate in 2050, water stress, how safely a foreigner can own farmland, political stability and wildfire, scored by the author's own reading. Inside the US, ownership and stability barely vary, so floods/storms/quakes and community take their slots, and every score comes from data.

Community resilience scores remote rural counties low (fewer services, thinner institutions). Turn it off to see land on physical risk alone.

Prices: the original uses average farmland per hectare (Eurostat, CREA, USDA, others), read here off its log axis and converted at the ECB rate. Its Central Valley (~$13.6K/ac) matches USDA's Fresno, Tulare and Merced ($15.6–16.5K). Its Central Coast (~$38.5K/ac) is well above USDA's Monterey, San Luis Obispo and Santa Barbara ($12–21K), likely irrigated cropland.
