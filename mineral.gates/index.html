How this is built

Scores are judgment, not data. Each score translates a policy event into the scale above. Pre-2015 scores reflect China's export quotas and duties on rare earths, tungsten, antimony, indium and graphite. The WTO ruled against them in 2014, and China removed them in 2015.

Key events. Sep 2010 rare-earth halt to Japan. Jan/May 2015 quotas and duties removed. Aug 2023 Ga/Ge licensing. Dec 2023 graphite licensing. Sep 2024 Sb licensing. Dec 2024 US ban on Ga/Ge/Sb. Feb 2025 W/Bi/In/Te licensing. Apr 2025 seven-rare-earth licensing (never suspended). Oct 2025 expansion. Nov 2025 truce suspends the US ban and the Oct 2025 package.

Scheduled (dotted). What happens if every rule runs on its written dates and nothing new is agreed. Two suspensions roll off. On Nov 10, 2026 the Oct 2025 package returns (Dy/Tb/Sm/Y/Sc 9, NdPr 7, graphite 7). On Nov 27, 2026 the US ban returns (Ga/Ge/Sb 10). Nothing on the books is scheduled to come down. The Feb 2025 tungsten/bismuth/indium/tellurium licensing and the Apr 2025 rare-earth licensing have no end date. The tungsten and antimony exporter lists run to end-2027 and are expected to be renewed.

Likely (dashed). My probability-weighted view, with renewal risk each year: score = renewed + p × (scheduled − renewed).

  • Oct 2025 rare-earth and graphite package: p = 45% from Nov 2026, rising to 65% from Nov 2027. Rare earths are Beijing's strongest lever and the first it reuses.
  • US-specific Ga/Ge/Sb ban: p = 30% from Dec 2026, rising to 50% from Dec 2027. It is the most visible escalation, so the least likely to be restored first.
  • Everything else is held flat: licensing is now a permanent tool. These probabilities are judgment. The US-side DoD tungsten ban (Jan 1, 2027) is not scored here.

Weights. Market value = 2025 world production × 2025 average price, held constant: Sb $6.1B, W $5.9B, NdPr $5.4B, graphite $0.9B, Dy $0.9B, Bi $0.7B, Ge $0.6B, In $0.5B, Ga $0.5B, Tb $0.4B, Te $0.15B, Y $0.14B, Sc $0.05B, Sm $0.01B. Mostly China-side prices, so heavy rare earths are underweighted relative to ex-China value. Cobalt and titanium sponge have no Chinese export gate and are excluded.
