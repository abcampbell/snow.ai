    What the cross-country comparison can support

    Bohai adds Tangshan and Tianjin against a changing single US port; the delta adds Shanghai and Ningbo against LA and Long Beach; Shenzhen and Guangzhou are compared with NY/NJ. Port boundaries and handling counts differ. USACE tonnage is commonly in short tons, while this ledger labels metric tonnes; the inherited conversions require source-level verification.

    China’s road series changes coverage in 2005 and is spliced as an index, whereas the US series counts surfaced roads. Total network length also differs from Sun’s macadam specification. Tianjin municipality and LA County have different boundaries. Xinjiang Han and Northeast population stocks are proxies for settlement flows, and the same ten-million Northwestern proposal is reused twice; Xinjiang cotton is compared with the whole US crop. River freight coverage and counting also require harmonization. The official 2000 road census reports 1,679,800 km, against 1,402,700 km in the inherited series; the road target date remains provisional until that coverage change is reconciled.

    The sediment-halving target is imputed. Sun described two parts of flood prevention; he gave no numerical sediment-reduction target. Lower sediment is treated as better in this index, with ecological effects outside its scope. The 1924 hydro target supplements the 1921 plan. Pooled results describe this selected index; source harmonization remains necessary before broader claims about national development.

    Sources and caveats

    Series are stitched from the National Bureau of Statistics yearbooks (rail, roads, steel, cement, hydro), the China Railway and Ministry of Transport year-end releases, port authority releases (Tangshan, Tianjin, Shanghai, Ningbo-Zhoushan, Hong Kong, Shenzhen, Guangzhou), the Yellow River Conservancy Commission’s Tongguan decadal sediment record, the national forest inventories, census returns for Xinjiang, the Northeast and the port cities, and Maddison’s 1990 international-dollar GDP series (extended past 2008 with real growth). Points before 1949, and especially before 1920, are scholarly estimates with wide error bars; they are marked ≈ in the data tables.

    Sun’s targets. Dashed lines are figures in his own text, page numbers from the 1922 Putnam edition: 100,000 miles of rail and 1,000,000 miles of road (p. 6), three ports with the capacity of New York Harbor (p. 7), ten million settlers in ten years (p. 24), a port city the size of Philadelphia within forty years, “not to say New York” (p. 16), $500 M–1 B Mex for iron and steel (p. 29), $100–200 M Mex for cement (p. 75), $300 M Mex for the Yangtze (p. 54), and 30 million horsepower from the gorges, his 1924 lecture figure, about 22 GW. Dotted lines and bands are inferred: at 1920 exchange rates (about 2 Mex to the gold dollar) and 1920 US plant costs (roughly $60–100 gold per annual tonne of steel, $12–18 per tonne of cement) his capital sums buy about 5–10 Mt and 3–8 Mt a year; New York Harbor moved about 45 million tons by steamship in 1921; Suez carried about 20 million tons. Treat the inferred figures as order-of-magnitude.

    The composite. Each series is interpolated to annual values in log10 between its data points, then z-scored against its own mean and standard deviation over the years it covers. Lower-is-better series (Yellow River silt) are sign-flipped. Every series is carried back to 1875 and held at the −3 floor before the thing existed (railways before 1876, containers before 1956, Xinjiang cotton and Han population before recorded coverage in 1949, where the early values are unknown), and carried forward at its last value to 2025 where the latest figure is a 2020 census or a 2024 release, so the composite always averages all sixteen and its shape is movement rather than composition; floor and carried-forward years are excluded from each series’ mean and standard deviation; interpolated annual values inside the observation span are included. Port sums end in the last year covered by every component, then the aggregate is carried forward. Sun’s bar is the mean of the z-scores his sixteen targets would have, computed the same way. Where he gave no figure at all the target is imputed, labeled so in the table, and the bar is also shown without those. All z-scores are winsorized at ±3; this is an index, not a measurement.

    The American run uses the same sixteen measures with US series (BTS and ICC route-miles, FHWA surfaced mileage, USGS steel and cement, EIA hydro, Army Corps waterborne commerce, census regional populations, Maddison), standardized over 1875–2025 exactly as China’s. “Pooled” standardizes each measure against both countries’ combined record, which puts the paired measures on a shared numerical scale; their definitions and geographic coverage still differ. Absolute target proxies are applied unchanged. The ten-million Northwestern settlement proposal is adapted to two population stocks and to American 1920 baselines. Sediment halving is an imputed target applied to each river’s historical baseline; the text gives no numerical reduction target. Pre-1900 US port and river tonnages are rough.

    Definition checks (v009). The official 2000 road census conflicts with the inherited road point. FHWA separates surface types; USACE defines port areas and reports short tons. See Sun’s 1922 text for the Northwestern settlement proposal and the two parts of flood prevention. Other inherited anchors still need point-level source verification.

    The era split attributes each measure’s increase to the era in which it occurred; 1949 rail is the nominal 13,500 miles (about 6,800 ran). Road statistics break in 2005, when village roads were added; the two calibers are plotted separately and the composite carries the old caliber forward at the new caliber’s growth rate.
