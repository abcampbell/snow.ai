Lithium

The lightest metal, light enough to float on water. Every battery chemistry in wide use needs it. The U.S. has large deposits in Nevada, Arkansas and North Carolina and only one producing operation.

Melting point
180.5 °C
Density
0.534 g/cm³
Sources
Spodumene, brines, clays
U.S. import reliance
>25%

What it's for

Uses

  • BatteriesCathodes and electrolytes in EVs, phones and grid storage.
  • Ceramics & glassGlass-ceramic cooktops and heat-resistant glass.
  • GreasesLithium soaps in high-temperature lubricating greases.
  • AlloysAluminum–lithium alloys in aircraft and spacecraft.

Pressure points

  1. 1950s–80sNorth Carolina's tin–spodumene belt is the world's leading source of lithium.
  2. 1966Lithium brine production begins at Silver Peak, Nevada.
  3. 2023Construction starts on a clay lithium mine at Thacker Pass, Nevada.

Our approach

The United States has lithium. What it lacks is conversion capacity, the plants that turn spodumene and brine into battery-grade carbonate and hydroxide. That is where we put our first dollar.
