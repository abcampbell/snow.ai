Antimony

A brittle grey metalloid that keeps plastics from burning and makes ammunition fire. The United States has mined almost none of it since the early 2000s.

Melting point
630.6 °C
Density
6.68 g/cm³
Main ore
Stibnite, Sb₂S₃
U.S. import reliance
≈85%

What it's for

Uses

  • Flame retardantsAntimony trioxide in plastics, textiles, cable sheathing and electronics housings.
  • DefensePrimers, tracer rounds, and hardened lead for small-arms ammunition.
  • Lead-acid batteriesHardens the lead grid in starter and backup batteries.
  • Solar glassA fining agent that clears bubbles from photovoltaic cover glass.

Who controls supply

The gap

Pressure points

  1. 1940sStibnite, Idaho supplies much of the antimony and tungsten the U.S. uses in the Second World War.
  2. Sep 2024China requires export licenses for antimony ores, metal and oxides.
  3. Dec 2024China bans exports of antimony, gallium and germanium to the United States.
  4. Nov 2025China suspends the U.S. ban for one year. Export licensing stays in place.

Our approach

Antimony usually turns up alongside gold and tungsten. We look first at districts that produced it in wartime and were abandoned when prices fell, and at old tailings that still carry it.
